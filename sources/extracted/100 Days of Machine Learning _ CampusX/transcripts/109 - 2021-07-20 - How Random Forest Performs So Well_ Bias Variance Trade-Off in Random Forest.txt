Basically, in machine learning, you get errors. Your machine learning models produce errors. Right? So, that error is composed of two things. One is bias and the other is variance. Right? What is bias? Bias is what we call it when a machine learning model cannot provide good results on training data. Right? Meaning, you have a machine learning model. It didn't learn very well during the training process itself. Meaning, it won't give good results on the training data. This means its bias will be high. Right? The second is variance. What is variance? Sometimes your machine learning model learns so well on the training data that it memorizes it. Basically, it memorizes the patterns of the training data. Due to which, when you give it new data, its performance isn't that good. So basically, if you make even slight changes to the data, your machine learning model's outcome won't be good. This is called high variance. Right? So, in machine learning, you must always try to keep your bias low. I will write it here. You always want low bias and low variance. But there is a problem. The problem is that there is an inverse relationship between these two, between bias and variance. Meaning, if you try to reduce one, the other increases. Right? And that is why most machine learning algorithms struggle with the bias-variance tradeoff. When you go to reduce one error, the other one increases. That is why most of the machine learning algorithms you have are defined into two classes. Right? The first class is low-bias, high-variance algorithms. Meaning, an algorithm that is able to give very good results on the training data. But is not able to provide as good results on new test data. An example of these algorithms is a fully grown decision tree. A decision tree where you don't cap the maximum depth. Right? A fully grown decision tree. Second, SVMs sometimes show this behavior, as do KNNs when the number of nearest neighbors is very low. Then there is a second class of algorithms where you have high bias but low variance. A very good example of this is linear regression and, in certain cases, logistic regression as well. But you never really get the combination you want. Where both the bias and the variance are low. This is where Random Forest helps you. The specialty of Random Forest is that if you give it a low-bias, high-variance algorithm, it converts it into a low-bias, low-variance algorithm. This means that for such algorithms, Random Forest keeps the bias as it is while reducing the variance. Right? Now let's understand how this happens. Right? So the answer lies in its working. Right?
